SECTION A: APPLIED CRYPTOGRAPHIC PROTOCOLS (40 MARKS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. (10 Marks) Explain why encrypting a document with RSA alone is inefficient for 
   large files. Contrast RSA-OAEP with hybrid cryptosystems combining RSA-OAEP 
   and AES-256-GCM in terms of computational complexity, key encapsulation, and 
   security guarantees.

2. (10 Marks) In the Galois/Counter Mode (AES-GCM), describe the critical role of 
   the Nonce (Initialization Vector) and Authentication Tag. What catastrophic 
   vulnerabilities arise if a nonce is reused under the same secret key?

3. (10 Marks) Explain the mathematical and security distinctions between PKCS#1 v1.5 
   signature padding and the Probabilistic Signature Scheme (RSA-PSS). Why does 
   modern cryptography mandate PSS for digital signature generation?

4. (10 Marks) In authenticated data transfer, define Authenticated Additional Data 
   (AAD). Why is it essential to bind non-confidential metadata (such as sender ID, 
   recipient ID, and file size) to the ciphertext using AAD during AES-GCM operations?

SECTION B: PRACTICAL SECURITY ENGINEERING (40 MARKS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. (15 Marks) Consider an untrusted communication channel where an adversary can 
   intercept, modify, and replay messages:
   (a) How does an RSA-PSS signature over the canonical JSON package protect integrity?
   (b) Does digital signature verification alone prevent replay attacks? Detail two 
       practical mechanisms (e.g., cryptographic nonces, synchronized timestamps, or 
       persistent message sequence registries) to eliminate replay vulnerability.

6. (15 Marks) Design a secure key storage protocol for an examination coordinator:
   (a) Why must RSA private keys be protected with PKCS#8 encrypted serialization 
       (using PBKDF2/scrypt key derivation) rather than stored in plaintext PEM?
   (b) Outline the public-key distribution dilemma and explain how out-of-band SHA-256 
       SubjectPublicKeyInfo fingerprints establish key authenticity without a full PKI.

7. (10 Marks) Describe the principle of "Fail-Closed" in cryptographic engineering. 
   Why must a secure receiver discard partial plaintext and refuse file creation 
   when signature verification, GCM authentication, or SHA-256 checks fail?

SECTION C: PROTOCOL ANALYSIS & ATTACK SCENARIOS (20 MARKS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. (20 Marks) Analyze the following attack vectors against the SecureExamExchange protocol:
   (a) Ciphertext Bit-Flipping: What prevents an attacker from flipping bits in the 
       ciphertext to alter examination questions?
   (b) Recipient ID Substitution: If Alice encrypts for Bob, but Eve re-labels the 
       recipient ID to Charlie, at what stage does the receiver detect this alteration?
   (c) Key Separation: Why must the sender's signing key and receiver's encryption key 
       remain completely separate cryptographic entities?
